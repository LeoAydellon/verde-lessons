Lesson 1.1 — Agriculture All Around You

Work through this on your own. Do each step, answer the questions, and turn in your work.

🌱 What this lesson is about

When people hear "agriculture" they picture a farm. But agriculture is much bigger than that — it's how a country feeds itself, clothes itself, and even powers itself. Almost everything you touched today started with agriculture: your breakfast, your cotton shirt, the leather in a pair of boots, even some of the fuel in a truck.

Big idea: agriculture touches food, energy, the environment, and the economy — all at once. A country that can feed its own people is a country that can take care of itself.

Food security = a country's people can reliably get enough safe, affordable food. Agriculture is what makes that possible.

📖 The words you'll use

  • Agriculture — the whole system of growing plants and raising animals for food, fiber, and other products.
  • Agribusiness — all the businesses connected to agriculture (not just farms — trucking, processing, seed companies, feed stores).
  • Food security — when people can reliably get enough safe food. The opposite is food insecurity.
  • Fiber — plant or animal material used to make things like clothing (cotton, wool).
  • By-product — a useful extra product made while producing something else (leather from beef cattle, cottonseed oil from cotton).
  • Biofuel — fuel made from plants (like ethanol made from corn).
  • Commodity — a raw agricultural product that's bought and sold, like corn, beef, milk, or cotton.

📋 Project 1.1.3 — Why Food Security Matters

Here's a real Arizona fact: in the winter, most of the lettuce and leafy greens eaten in the entire United States are grown in Yuma, Arizona. One region, feeding a whole country for a season.

Write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences) answering this: What could happen if a country could NOT grow enough of its own food and had to depend on other countries for most of it? Think about:

  • What happens to prices when food has to travel farther or gets scarce?
  • What happens if the countries you depend on have a drought — or a disagreement with you?
  • Why might a strong farm sector matter even to people who live in cities?
