Lesson 3.3 — Risk and Opportunity

Work through this on your own. Do each step, answer the questions, and turn in your work.

🌱 What this lesson is about

Last lesson you learned that starting a business means giving up a paycheck. But you also give up other things: the money you invest could have earned interest somewhere else, and your skill at managing is worth something too. This lesson adds those up so you can see the full trade-off — and then judge whether an opportunity is actually worth it.

Big idea: every dollar and every hour you put into your business could have gone somewhere else. Comparing those "somewhere elses" is how you find the best opportunity.

📖 The three opportunity costs

  • Opportunity cost of capital — the return your money could have earned elsewhere (like interest in a savings account) instead of being tied up in the business.
  • Opportunity cost of labor — the wages your time could have earned at another job.
  • Opportunity cost of management — what your decision-making skill could have earned managing something else.

And two ways to measure your reward:

  • Return on assets (ROA) — profit compared to everything the business owns (from Lesson 2.2).
  • Return on equity (ROE) — profit compared to the money you personally put in. It shows how hard your own money is working.

🧮 Activity 3.3.1 — The Cost of Capital

You have $5,000 to invest. You could put it in a safe savings account earning 4% a year, or put it into your business.

  1. How much would the $5,000 earn in one year in the savings account (5,000 × 0.04)?
  2. That amount is your opportunity cost of capital — the safe return you're giving up. Write it down.
  3. Your business is expected to earn you $600 on that $5,000 this year. Is that more or less than the savings account would have earned?
Answer: If the business earns less than the safe savings account, why might someone still choose the business anyway?

⚖️ Activity 3.3.2 — Capital, Labor & Management Together

Total opportunity cost?
  1. Add up the total opportunity cost.

Answer: Did running the business "beat" all the other things you could have done with your money and time? Explain using your number.

📈 Activity 3.3.3 — Return on Equity (ROE)

ROE shows how hard your own money is working in the business.

ROE = Net income ÷ Your invested money × 100 (a percent)

Two owners:

Net incomeTheir own money investedROE = ?
Owner A$1,000$5,000
Owner B$1,000$10,000
Answer: Both made $1,000. Whose money is working harder, and how do you know from the ROE?

📋 Project 3.3.4 — Weigh Your Own Opportunity

For the business idea you've been planning:

  • Estimate your opportunity cost of capital (money you'd invest × a safe 4%).
  • Estimate your opportunity cost of labor (your hours × a pay rate).
  • Add a rough management cost.
  • Add them for a total opportunity cost, and compare it to what you expect your business to give you.
Decide: Based on your numbers, is your business worth doing over the other options? Write two or three sentences making your case.
