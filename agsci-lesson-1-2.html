Unit 1 · Agriscience III

Lesson 1.2 — Buying, Selling & Working: Agriculture and the Economy

Work through this on your own. Do each step, answer the questions, and turn in your work.

🌱 What this lesson is about

Countries trade farm products the same way you might trade lunch items — you trade away what you have plenty of, and trade for what you can't easily get. The United States grows more of some crops than it can use, so it sells them to other countries. And some things (like bananas or coffee) barely grow here at all, so we buy them from other countries.

Big idea: when a country exports farm products, money flows in from other countries — and that trade, plus everything agriculture touches, creates jobs far beyond the farm.

Export = we sell it to another country (money comes in). Import = we buy it from another country (money goes out).

📖 The words you'll use

  • Export — a product sold to another country. (U.S. soybeans shipped overseas.)
  • Import — a product bought from another country. (Bananas from Central America.)
  • Trade — the buying and selling of goods between people, businesses, or countries.
  • Foreign exchange — money earned from other countries when we sell them our products.
  • Labor force — all the people working (or looking for work) in an economy.
  • Ag career pathway — any job that exists because of agriculture, on the farm or far from it.

🚢 Activity 1.2.1 — Import or Export?

For each product, decide: is the United States most likely exporting it (selling to other countries) or importing it (buying from other countries)? Copy the list and label each one:

  1. Soybeans grown in the Midwest, shipped to Asia
  2. Bananas in the grocery store
  3. Coffee beans for a coffee shop
  4. Arizona-grown winter lettuce sold to Canada
  5. Chocolate (cacao) for candy bars
  6. American beef sold to Japan
  7. Avocados in January
Then answer in your own words: Why doesn't the U.S. just grow its own coffee and bananas instead of importing them? (Hint: think about climate.)

💵 Activity 1.2.2 — Where the Money Flows

Read this short story, then answer the questions:

An Arizona ranch sells 200 head of cattle. Some of that beef is exported to Japan. Japan pays in money that comes back to the U.S. — to the ranch, the trucking company, the feed store, and the processing plant. The ranch uses its share to buy a new stock trailer from a dealer in Phoenix.

  1. Where did new money enter this story from outside the country?
  2. List every business in the story that touched that money.
  3. What do you think happens to those businesses if the export deal disappears next year?
Big picture: In one sentence, explain why selling farm products to other countries helps people who don't farm at all.

📋 Project 1.2.3 — The Jobs Web

Agriculture creates jobs everywhere — most of them are not on a farm. Make a list of eight different jobs that exist because of agriculture. Rules:

  • No more than two can be jobs done on a farm or ranch.
  • For each job, write one sentence on how it connects to agriculture.
  • Ideas to get you started (don't just copy these): veterinarian, livestock truck driver, food safety inspector, welder building gates and trailers, feed store clerk, drone operator mapping fields, food scientist, ag teacher.
Wrap it up: Circle (or mark) the one job from your list you'd most want — and write one sentence on why.
