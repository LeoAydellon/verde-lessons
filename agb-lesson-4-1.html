Unit 4 · Agribusiness

Lesson 4.1 — Finalizing the Plan

This is the capstone. You'll pull everything together into a finished business plan and pitch it.

🌱 What this lesson is about

You've learned to track money, measure a business's health, plan for risk, and weigh opportunities. Now you put it all together into a finished business plan — the document you'd show an investor or a bank to get support. You'll size up your competition, set clear goals, build a timeline, and pitch your idea.

Big idea: a business plan turns a good idea into something other people can believe in and back with money.

📖 The words you'll use

  • SWOT — a four-part look at your business: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.
  • Competitive product — someone else's product that competes with yours for the same customers.
  • Complementary product — a product that helps yours sell (hot dogs help sell buns).
  • Competitive analysis — sizing up your competition to see how you stack up.
  • SMART goal — a goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-based.
  • Milestone — a checkpoint on your timeline (a date by which something should be done).
  • Pitch — a short, convincing presentation of your business to someone who might invest.

🔍 Project 4.1.1 — SWOT Analysis

Fill in a SWOT for the business you've been planning. Two boxes are about you (inside the business), two are about the outside world.

Answer: Look at your weaknesses and threats — pick the most serious one and describe how you'd deal with it.

🏪 Activity 4.1.2 — Sizing Up the Competition

Think about who else sells something like what you'll sell.

  1. Name 2 competitors (other stands, stores, or sellers your customers could choose instead).
  2. For each, write one thing they do better than you and one thing you can do better than them.
  3. Name 1 complementary product or business — something that could help your sales.
Answer: What is the one reason a customer should pick you over your competition? (This is your edge — say it in one sentence.)

🎯 Project 4.1.3 — Set a SMART Goal

A weak goal is "sell a lot." A SMART goal is "sell 200 jars of honey by the end of October at $8 each." Write one SMART goal for your business and check each part:

SMART partDoes your goal have it?
Specific — exactly what?
Measurable — a number to check?
Achievable — realistic?
Relevant — matters to your business?
Time-based — has a deadline?
Write it: State your final SMART goal in one clear sentence.

📅 Project 4.1.4 — Build a Timeline

A timeline lists the milestones that get you to your goal. Make a simple one with at least 4 milestones and a date for each:

Milestone (what gets done)By when (date)
Example: buy suppliesExample: March 1
Answer: Which milestone is the most important to hit on time, and what happens to the rest of the plan if you miss it?

📋 Project 4.1.5 — Compile Your Business Plan

Now bring together the work you've done across these lessons into one finished plan. It should include:

  • A short description of your business (what you sell and to whom).
  • Your income & expenses and predicted net income (Lessons 2.1–2.4).
  • Your balance sheet / net worth (Lesson 2.3).
  • Your risk management plan (Lesson 3.1).
  • Your SWOT, competitive analysis, SMART goal, and timeline (this lesson).
Check: Read your plan as if you were an investor. Is there anything missing or unclear? Fix it.

🎤 Project 4.1.6 — Pitch It

A pitch is a short, convincing summary you'd give to someone who might invest. Write a pitch of about 5–7 sentences that covers:

  • What your business is and the problem it solves.
  • Why it will make money (use a number from your plan).
  • Your edge over the competition.
  • What you're asking for (money, support) and what the investor gets back.
Finish strong: End your pitch with one sentence that would make someone want to say "yes."
