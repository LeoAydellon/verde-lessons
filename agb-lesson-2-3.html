Lesson 2.3 — Measuring Success

Work through this on your own. Do each step, answer the questions, and turn in your work.

🌱 What this lesson is about

"Success" is more than just making a sale. A healthy business is one that owns more than it owes and can pay its bills when they come due. In this lesson you'll learn to read a balance sheet — a snapshot of everything a business owns and owes on one day — and use it to judge how healthy a business really is.

Big idea: what you own minus what you owe = what the business is really worth to you.

📖 The words you'll use

  • Assets — everything the business owns (cash, tools, land, animals, inventory).
  • Liabilities — everything the business owes (loans, unpaid bills).
  • Net worth (owner's equity) — Assets − Liabilities. What's truly yours.
  • Balance sheet — a one-page snapshot listing all assets, all liabilities, and net worth on a certain date.
  • Current — will turn into cash or come due within a year (cash, this year's bills).
  • Noncurrent — lasts or is owed longer than a year (land, a multi-year loan).
  • Liquidity — can the business pay its short-term bills right now?
  • Solvency — can the business pay everything it owes if it had to?

💧 Activity 2.3.2 — Liquidity (Can it pay bills now?)

Liquidity asks: does the business have enough current assets to cover its current liabilities?

Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities

A ratio above 1.0 means it can cover its short-term bills. Below 1.0 means it can't — that's a warning sign.

Answer: Which stand can pay its short-term bills, and which one is in trouble? How can you tell from the ratio?

🏦 Activity 2.3.3 — Solvency (Can it pay everything?)

Solvency looks at the whole business — all debts against all assets.

Debt-to-asset ratio = Total liabilities ÷ Total assets

This tells you what fraction of the business is owed to lenders. Lower is healthier — it means more of the business is actually yours. (For example, 0.30 means 30% is owed and 70% is yours.)

Two businesses:

Total liabilitiesTotal assetsDebt-to-asset = ?
Business X$3,000$10,000
Business Y$8,000$10,000
Answer: Which business owns more of itself and is on safer ground? Explain using the ratios.

📋 Project 2.3.4 — Your Business's Balance Sheet

For the business idea you've been planning, make a simple balance sheet:

  • List at least 4 assets (what your business owns) with a dollar value for each.
  • List at least 2 liabilities (what it owes) with a dollar value for each.
  • Add up each side and calculate your net worth (assets − liabilities).
  • Calculate your current ratio if you can.
Wrap it up: Based on your numbers, is your business healthy? Write two sentences using the words net worth, liquidity, or solvency.
