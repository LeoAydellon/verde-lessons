Lesson 2.1 — Categorizing Transactions

Work through this on your own. Do each step, answer the questions, and turn in your work.

🌱 What this lesson is about

Every business — a farm, a shop, or your own idea — has money coming in and money going out. To run a business well, you have to track those and sort them into the right buckets. That's what today is about.

Big idea: if you know your income and your expenses, you can figure out whether you're actually making money.

Net income = Income − Expenses. If it's positive, you made a profit. If it's negative, you lost money.

📖 The words you'll use

  • Income — money coming into the business (like selling a product).
  • Expense — money the business spends to operate (like buying supplies).
  • Net income — what's left after you subtract expenses from income.
  • Transaction — any single exchange of money (one sale, one purchase).
  • Ledger — the running list where you write every transaction down.
  • Cash vs. Non-cash — cash = money changes hands now; non-cash = value changes without cash moving (like something wearing out over time).
  • Fixed expense — stays the same each month (like rent).
  • Variable expense — changes based on how much you do (like feed for more animals).
  • Current vs. Noncurrent asset — current = used up within a year; noncurrent = lasts more than a year (like a tractor).

🧮 Activity 2.1.1 — Income or Expense?

Decide whether each item is income (money in) or an expense (money out). Copy the list and label each one:

  1. You sell a dozen eggs at the farmers market.
  2. You buy a bag of chicken feed.
  3. You pay the electric bill for the barn.
  4. A customer pays you to mow their field.
  5. You buy fuel for the tractor.
  6. You get paid for a load of hay you sold.
Then answer in your own words: If your income for the month was $500 and your expenses were $350, what was your net income — and did the business make money?

🗂 Activity 2.1.2 — Classifying Expenses

Every expense fits a type. Sort each expense below into Fixed, Variable, or Noncurrent asset:

Explain: Why is a tractor a "noncurrent asset" and not just a regular expense? (Hint: how long does it last?)

📋 Project 2.1.3 — Your Business's Income & Expenses

Think about the business idea you've been planning. Make two lists:

  • Income: at least 3 ways your business could make money.
  • Expenses: at least 5 things your business would have to pay for — and label each Fixed, Variable, or Noncurrent asset.
Wrap it up: Do you think your business would have a positive net income? Write one or two sentences explaining why or why not.
