Lesson 2.4 — Business Decisions

🌱 What this lesson is about

Now you can read the numbers — this lesson is about using them to make smart choices. Two tools help you decide: an income statement (what already happened over a period of time) and a budget (your best guess at what will happen next). Good managers use both to decide whether a plan is worth doing.

Big idea: an income statement looks backward at what you earned; a budget looks forward at what you plan to earn. Comparing the two tells you if you're on track.

📖 The words you'll use

  • Income statement — a summary of all income and all expenses over a period (a month, a season, a year), ending in the net income.
  • Net operating income — income left after the normal costs of running the business. Your bottom line for that period.
  • Budget — a plan that predicts future income and expenses before they happen.
  • Enterprise — one specific money-making activity (for example, "the egg operation" or "the pumpkin patch").
  • Enterprise budget — a budget for just one enterprise, so you can see if that one activity is worth it.
  • Fiscal year — the 12-month period a business uses for its accounting.
  • Feasible — worth doing; the plan is likely to make money.

🧮 Activity 2.4.1 — Incoming and Outgoing (Income Statement)

An income statement lists income at the top, expenses below, and subtracts to find net income. Build one for a season of a small pumpkin stand:

Hayride tickets$600
ExpensesAmount

  1. Add up total income.
  2. Add up total expenses.
  3. Net operating income = total income − total expenses. What is it?
  4. Did the stand make a profit this season?

📈 Activity 2.4.2 — Building a Budget

A budget predicts next season before it happens. Suppose you plan to grow the pumpkin stand next year: you expect income to go up 25%, but supply costs to go up too.

Predicted net income?
  1. What is next year's predicted income (last year's $3,000 plus 25%)?
  2. Add up next year's predicted expenses.
  3. What is the predicted net income for next year?
Answer: Is growing the stand a good idea based on your budget? Would you do it? Why?

🎲 Activity 2.4.3 — Applying Business Decisions

Real managers use their numbers to choose between options. You have $1,000 to invest in one new enterprise. Here are two choices:

Option A: Egg layersOption B: Cut flowers
Startup cost$1,000$1,000
Expected income$1,700$2,200
Expected expenses$500$1,100
RiskLow (steady demand)Higher (spoils fast)
  1. Find the predicted net income for each option (income − expenses).
  2. Which option makes more money on paper?
  3. Which would you actually choose once you consider the risk? Explain.
Answer: A budget shows Option B earns more, but it's riskier. Explain in your own words why the "best on paper" choice isn't always the right one.

📋 Project 2.4.4 — A Budget for Your Business

For the business idea you've been planning, write a one-year budget:

  • Predict your total income for the year (be realistic).
  • List and add up your expected expenses.
  • Calculate your predicted net income.
Decide: Based on your budget, is your business feasible — worth doing? Write two or three sentences making your case with the numbers.
